FLAGSHIP CASE STUDY · RETROSPECTIVE MODEL EVALUATION

IEEE-CIS Fraud Detection

Can a model improve the ranking of rare fraudulent transactions when investigation capacity is limited?

I compared a fixed Logistic Regression baseline with XGBoost on transaction-only data, using a chronological model-training, calibration and final-evaluation design. The study demonstrates evidence-based model evaluation—not a production fraud service.

03 / METHOD

Chronology before
model selection.

The primary comparison separates model fitting, calibration assessment and final evaluation in transaction time, rather than randomly mixing observations across the time axis. This avoids training on later records while evaluating earlier ones and asks how ranking looks on a later cohort; it does not establish future operational performance.

Dataset and scope

  • Full labeled IEEE-CIS transaction table: 590,540 rows, 392 predictors and 20,663 frauds (3.499001% prevalence).
  • Target: isFraud; row identifier TransactionID excluded from predictors.
  • Identity tables were not merged. The unlabeled competition test table (506,691 rows) was checked for input-contract and provenance only; it was not used as a scored validation set.
  • Transforms and estimators were fitted on model-training rows only. No sampling was used.

Model comparison

  • Unweighted Logistic Regression with training-only scaling, used as a fixed baseline.
  • XGBoost: 500 estimators, depth 6, learning rate 0.05, row/column subsampling 0.8, histogram tree method, seed 42 and four CPU threads.
  • The same compact ordinal feature representation constrains the linear model: nominal codes have arbitrary geometry, and it was not an optimized one-hot baseline.
  • No broad hyperparameter search, identity enrichment or alternative-score selection was used.

04 / INTERPRETATION

Evidence with
boundaries.

  • Retrospective only. One later temporal cohort; no online intervention, independently validated deployment, staffing policy, monetary savings or regulatory suitability is established.
  • Feature timing and label maturation are uncertain. Anonymized fields lack a verified point-in-time availability contract, so no blanket leakage-free claim is made.
  • Not entity-disjoint. Repeated card-field combinations are unverified attribute proxies, not customer or account identifiers; unseen-entity generalization was not tested.
  • Limited robustness evidence. No bootstrap confidence interval or second empirical split was run. The final metrics describe one cohort and may not transfer under temporal drift.
  • Model representation matters. Logistic Regression is a deliberately simple ordinal-code baseline, not an optimized categorical benchmark; its weak top-budget precision is reported as observed.
  • Evidence provenance has limits. Recorded file hashes identify bytes but do not independently certify data authenticity or authorization. Competition access and publication rules must be checked by the data user.

This is a retrospective research benchmark, not a production-ready fraud system. Batch precision is not a causal impact estimate and does not establish financial benefit.
