FLAGSHIP CASE STUDY · FRAUD RISK

IEEE-CIS Fraud Detection

Can a model help prioritize a limited review queue for rare fraudulent transactions?

Compared fixed Logistic Regression and XGBoost on transaction-only data using chronological model-training, calibration and final-evaluation partitions.

0.887546ROC-AUC · temporal holdout
0.480197Average precision · temporal holdout
0.870558Precision · top 1% batch review

Decision relevance: the fixed-capacity analysis makes the precision–recall trade-off explicit for a review queue; results are cohort-specific batch metrics, not a deployed policy or financial outcome.

PythonXGBoostTemporal evaluationCalibration
Evaluation design & limitations

The full labeled transaction table contains 590,540 rows and 392 predictors. The chronological design used 354,324 model-training rows, 118,108 calibration rows and 118,108 final-evaluation rows. On that same final partition, Logistic Regression reached ROC-AUC 0.805437 and average precision 0.173031; XGBoost reached 0.887546 and 0.480197. Average precision is the step-weighted precision–recall summary.

ROC-AUC describes ranking discrimination. At a fixed top-1% batch budget, XGBoost issued 1,182 alerts, identified 1,029 fraud cases, and recorded precision 0.870558 and recall 0.253199. This is a retrospective cohort scenario, not a staffing policy or financial outcome.

Anonymized feature decision-time availability and label maturity remain uncertain. Repeated card attributes are proxies, not verified customer IDs; the split is not entity-disjoint or production validation. Calibration-partition ECE stayed below the predeclared trigger, so no sigmoid calibrator was fitted.
